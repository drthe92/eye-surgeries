[Phacoemulsification for beginners]
For cortical removal, it's important to remember to move in an arc. We don't want to just pull centrally because that puts more stress on individual zonules. If you move in an arc and you have someone with pseudoexfoliation or weak zonules, it's less likely to cause them to tear and vitreous to prolapse around the capsule. Once I've engaged the cortex, I like to push down or posterior slightly, to create almost a U-shaped motion. We want to engage it by moving in an arc, then add foot as we move down, and peel it away from the anterior capsule before we pull it centrally. We can polish a little if we need to. Polishing is really about contact and rubbing the surface of the posterior capsule more than it is about irrigation/aspiration. We want to be real gentle on the foot there. Always, when using the I/A tip, if we're looking down the bore of the port, it's very unlikely to grab capsule. It's when we have it tilted to the side that we're at risk of grabbing posterior capsule or iris.

[Stop and chop]
The cortex here is very sticky, and one can use a side to side sweeping motion first to grasp the body of the cortical chunk, then pull this chunk to the safe zone and then aspirate it. This reduces the chance of catching the capsule and this is key to avoid a rent at this stage. This is the most common stage at which surgeons experience capsular tears and rents.
